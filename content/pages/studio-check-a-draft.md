A summary written after a meeting can quietly reverse who agreed to what. That is as true of a tired clinician's note as of a draft produced by an AI tool. This two-minute exercise practices one habit: read the draft against the source before you sign it.

## The source

In the rehearsal, Rowan says:

> “Wait for me to ask.”

## The draft

A prepared summary of the meeting reads:

> “Rowan agreed that Sal can answer for them.”

## Your turn

Say aloud what is wrong with the draft. Then say one sentence that matches the source.

<details>
<summary>Compare your revision</summary>

The draft reverses agency. It turns something Sal undertook into something Rowan conceded. One accurate revision:

> “Sal agreed to wait until Rowan asks for help.”

</details>

## What to check in any draft

Check every attribution, emotion, consent boundary, commitment and citation against the source. The person who signs the note owns it.

## For the facilitator

Check the learner's revision against the source line, not against the model answer. Nothing in this exercise listens, records, sends a message, calls a model or acts for a patient.
