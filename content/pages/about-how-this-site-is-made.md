## Who writes and who decides

During authoring, a faculty editor chooses the objective, the fictional source and the permitted content. A writing assistant can propose alternate learner lines or critique a fictional draft. The editor checks every attribution, emotion, consent boundary, commitment and citation against the source, revises the text, and owns the final version.

## What AI did in this build

- The six move cards, the rehearsal and the boundaries page were drafted with ChatGPT in a design brief dated November 15, 2026.
- Claude assembled the site from that brief, wrote the pages and the rehearsal branches that the governance page marks as its own, and checked each cited study against PubMed or the publisher's page.
- Claude adapted the unit workflow from the author's own one-page procedure, and assembled the setup page and the formats page from his teaching materials. The unit, its institution and the products it uses are not named.
- No AI system attested any page. Attestation is a faculty act, recorded with the reviewer's name, the date and a hash of the exact text.

## What the site itself does

It ships only authored text. It never listens, records, sends a message, calls a model or acts for a patient. There is no login and no analytics. Nothing typed or chosen on a page is stored or sent, and refreshing a rehearsal starts it over.

## Where the text comes from

Three pages are copied byte for byte from the Psychiatry Clerkship Library by Joshua Moss, MD. The [governance page](page:about/governance) lists each one with the commit it was taken from and the hash that proves it is unchanged. The six move cards, the rehearsal and the boundaries page come from the design brief. The unit workflow, its setup page and the formats page come from the author's own procedure and teaching. Everything else was written for this site.
