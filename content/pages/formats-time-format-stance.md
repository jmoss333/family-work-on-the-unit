How long should the contact be, and what is it for? This page gives the three-format menu Dr. Moss teaches residents. It is one clinician’s working rule, offered as a starting point.

## Three formats, titrated to stability

| Format | What it is for | When |
|---|---|---|
| 5-minute phone update | Collateral focus. The clinician gathers baseline. Zero relational processing. Protects fragile capacity and prevents acute escalation. | Start here when acuity is high |
| 30-minute structured meeting | Logistics and safety. A single concrete agenda and practical transition plans, strictly time-bounded. Deflects relational conflict. | Step up when sleep is restored (5–6 h) and arousal is down |
| 60–90-minute alliance repair (optional) | Relational dynamics: high expressed emotion, rupture processing, trauma and systemic repair, multi-party boundary work. | Step up when attention holds an agenda, and purpose, safety, and staffing support it |

**Decision rule:** the lowest-burden format that matches current acuity. Step up only as each gate is met; step down any time. None, separate contacts, or deferral remain available.

Format and purpose are separate decisions. A phone call can contain therapy; an in-person meeting may be coordination only. Receiving collateral never authorizes disclosure. Write down why you chose the format and what would change it.

The sleep figure is a local pacing convention, not an evidence-derived threshold: the number is there so the team makes the same call twice.

When a family can meet more than once, high expressed emotion becomes the target of the later meetings.

**No joint meeting today when:** suspected partner violence · suspected family abuse · the patient declines · contact exceeds tolerance. Ask privately; qualified interpreter, never a relative. The 5-minute phone update or separate contacts can be the plan.

## Five parts at any length

The 30 and the 60–90 share five parts: join each person → hear each experience → work on one exchange → agree what to try → close and review. [Two kinds of conversation, one spine](page:start/one-spine#the-five-parts-of-a-meeting) shows which move card goes with each part.

## The stance: PACE

- **Playfulness:** warmth and lightness when welcome
- **Acceptance:** acknowledge the experience; keep limits clear
- **Curiosity:** “help me understand” before you explain or correct
- **Empathy:** name what sounds painful; leave room
