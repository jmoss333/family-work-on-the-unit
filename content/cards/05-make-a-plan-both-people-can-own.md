Turn one new understanding into a small experiment. Ask each person what they are willing and able to do and how either could change their mind.

> “Rowan, what would give you room to answer? Sal, is that something you can do at the next visit? How would either of you call it off?”

## When to use or pause

Useful when each has identified a preferred change. If they disagree, record two preferences in the exercise and offer a smaller trial or none. Avoid assigning caregiving, housing, monitoring or availability to someone who has not agreed.

## Misstep and repair

“Sal will handle all the calls” converts concern into a duty. Try “I assigned you a job. What part, if any, would you choose? What remains the team’s responsibility?”

## Evidence and supervision

Problem-solving appears in longer family interventions \[D2, X2, G3\]. This single negotiated experiment is expert-informed. Have a supervisor distinguish willingness, capacity and available resources.
