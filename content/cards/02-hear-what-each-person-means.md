Invite each person to describe one moment. Check meaning with the speaker before moving to solutions.

> “What did you hope Sal would understand?” Then, “Sal, what was it like from your chair?” Back to the patient: “Which part of that fits, and what did we miss?”

## When to use or pause

Useful when people use the same word differently, or one person speaks for both. Offer an interpreter, more time, writing, or separate accounts if a person cannot easily join in.

## Misstep and repair

“You both just want what’s best” can erase a real disagreement. Try “I put one meaning on two experiences. What would each of you change about what I said?”

## Evidence and supervision

Direct inpatient qualitative evidence supports hearing difficult perspectives \[D1\]; equal-turn prompts are expert-informed. Ask an observer to identify whose meaning the clinician checked and whose remained untested.
