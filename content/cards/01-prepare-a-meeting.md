Privately ask whom the patient wants involved, what they hope to say and what can be shared. Offer a way to pause or decline.

> “Would you like anyone here? What would you like them to understand? Is there anything you want me to keep out of this conversation?”

## When to use or pause

Useful before invitations and again if the topic, attendees or format changes. Check what language or communication support each person needs. If privacy is unavailable, fear emerges, or participation is unclear, pause the invitation and arrange a private check.

## Misstep and repair

Calling a relative to arrange a meeting can itself reveal care. If the assumption is caught beforehand, stop and ask privately. If anything has already been disclosed, acknowledge what happened privately and involve the supervising clinician and privacy process.

## Evidence and supervision

Federal/Maine guidance informs disclosure. The private invitation is expert-informed practice; its wording has no standalone outcome trial. Rehearse with a supervisor and use a qualified interpreter when needed.
