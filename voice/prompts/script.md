Draft what the clinician says in one family meeting, as a script in five parts. It is a rehearsal aid for a supervised clinician and a starting point for Dr. Moss to edit. It is not a transcript: write only the clinician’s lines. The family’s words are theirs, and a meeting that follows a script word for word has stopped listening.

The meeting:

{{brief}}

The patient was asked privately before this meeting, and agreed to what is under “scope”. Stay inside it. A line that would open something outside the scope does not belong in the script.

Write the five parts in this order: `join`, `hear`, `exchange`, `agree`, `close`. They are the five parts every meeting on his site shares: join each person, hear each experience, work on one exchange, agree what to try, close and review.

For each part, write one to {{partLines}} lines. For each line:

- `to`: the name of the person it is said to, exactly as the brief has it, or “everyone”.
- `kind`: what the line does. One of: `ask` (a question that gives someone the floor), `reflect` (says back what was heard, tentatively), `frame` (one of his ideas, in plain words), `limit` (a short statement of what this meeting is for, or of who a task belongs to; say what is in, and leave what is out unnamed), `plan` (helps settle one small thing to try), `thanks` (names something a person did).
- `say`: the words.

Shape:

- Every part holds at least one `ask`.
- A {{format}}-minute meeting carries at most this many lines of kind `frame` in all: {{frames}}. Never two in one part. An idea takes the floor; spend it once.
- `join` speaks to each person in the room, by name, and says in one sentence what the meeting is for.
- `exchange` works on one moment between two people. It asks the listener what they heard before anyone explains, then gives the speaker room to correct it.
- `agree` settles one small thing each person is willing and able to do, and how either of them could change their mind.
- `close` asks what each person will take away, offers a last word, and leaves the next step open to a no.

`title`: a plain name for the meeting, for his files.
